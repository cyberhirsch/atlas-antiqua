# Ancient geography and linked data

Projects that link ancient places, maps, itineraries and roads.

- **[Pelagios Network](https://pelagios.org):** the main effort to link ancient
  maps, itineraries, and texts by tagging the places they mention against
  shared identifiers, so you can cross-reference sources like the Peutinger
  Map, Ptolemy, and the Antonine Itinerary.
- **[Pleiades](https://pleiades.stoa.org):** the gazetteer of ancient places
  that Pelagios and most others link to; effectively the common backbone.
  First data source of Atlas Antiqua (see `scripts/pleiades.py`).
- **[Digital Atlas of the Roman Empire (DARE)](https://imperium.ahlfeldt.se)
  and the Barrington Atlas:** modern maps that compile ancient sources into one
  geography.
- **[Vici.org](https://vici.org):** a community-edited archaeological atlas
  of classical antiquity: Roman sites, buildings, roads and finds on a map,
  many linked to Pleiades. Licence and export options still to check.
- **[Itiner-e](https://itiner-e.org):** a recent open dataset reconstructing the
  full Roman road network from many sources.
- **[ORBIS](https://orbis.stanford.edu) (Stanford):** a model of travel times
  and costs across the Roman world.
- **[Omnesviae](https://omnesviae.org):** a route planner built specifically on
  the Peutinger Map.
