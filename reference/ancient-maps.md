# Ancient maps and itineraries

Historical maps and route lists that could be georeferenced onto the atlas.
Dates are given here as in the sources (BC/AD); in the data they become HE.

## Closest relatives (Roman route maps and itineraries)

- **Dura-Europos shield map (3rd c. AD):** a painted fragment on a soldier's
  shield showing stations along the Black Sea coast, in a similar schematic
  road-map style.
- **Antonine Itinerary (3rd c. AD):** a written list of Roman roads and
  distances, essentially the Peutinger Map in text form.
- **Vicarello Cups:** four silver cups engraved with the stations and distances
  from Cádiz to Rome.
- **Bordeaux Itinerary (333 AD):** a pilgrim's route from Gaul to Jerusalem.

## Other ancient maps

- **Madaba Map (6th c. AD):** a floor mosaic in Jordan depicting the Holy Land,
  including a detailed Jerusalem.
- **Forma Urbis Romae (c. 205 AD):** a huge marble plan of the city of Rome,
  surviving in fragments.
- **Orange cadastres:** Roman land-survey maps carved in marble in southern
  France.
- **Ptolemy's Geography (2nd c. AD):** coordinates for thousands of places,
  later reconstructed into maps.
- **Babylonian Map of the World (c. 6th c. BC):** a clay tablet showing the
  world as a disc.
- **Turin Papyrus Map (c. 1150 BC):** an Egyptian map of a mining region, often
  called the oldest topographic map.
- **Mawangdui maps (2nd c. BC):** Han Chinese silk maps with surprisingly
  accurate terrain and garrisons.
